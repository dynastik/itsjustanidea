# itsjustanidea: Story Script (long, v3)

## The Last Delivery | 0.05-0.46 | calm
*Day 1. Warm, ordinary. Ends at sunset with the first Bellweather sign.*

The first parcel was easy. Mill Lane, a signature, a wave from the doorway. Elias climbed back into the van with the engine ticking and the whole afternoon still ahead of him.

The town fell away behind him. Green fields rolled toward distant hills, and clouds drifted lazily across the sky. One more delivery, and the day was his.

It had been an uneventful day, which was exactly how he liked his working days. No traffic jams, no angry customers, no packages sent to the wrong address. He had already decided what he would have for dinner. Something simple, with a window open.

The road curved gently between the fields. A farmhouse stood beyond a line of trees, smoke rising from its chimney. At a stop along the road, he checked the delivery sheet. The next address was still the same.

He passed a small roadside shop with flower boxes beneath its windows. A handwritten sign advertised fresh bread. For a moment he wondered whether he should have stopped. Then he laughed at himself and kept driving.

@act uneasy
The sun had begun to sink toward the hills when Elias saw a sign beside the road.

[gantry] WELCOME TO BELLWEATHER.

He didn't recognize the name, but that wasn't surprising. He had taken unfamiliar roads before. A smaller sign beneath it gave the distance to the town. Twelve miles. Twelve miles was nothing. He took the next exit, and the road folded him gently down toward the lights.

## Bellweather | 0.50-0.84 | uneasy
*Night 1. The sign repeats. The first message.*

Bellweather looked a great deal like the town he had left that morning, but all small towns look alike at dusk. He signed for the parcel, climbed back into the van, and rejoined the highway as the lamps came on.

The road bent around a wooded hill, passed a stretch of open farmland, and descended into a shallow valley. Then another sign appeared.

[gantry] WELCOME TO BELLWEATHER.

Elias frowned. The lettering was different, and the sign looked newer than the first. He checked the mirror. There had been no turn. No junction. No reason he could think of to have circled back.

A few minutes later, he passed a roadside milestone. Twelve miles to Bellweather. He had delivered in Bellweather less than an hour ago. He slowed slightly. The road ahead remained empty, and the last sunlight lay across the fields like a blanket.

Nothing looked wrong. That was what bothered him.

@act wrong
He pulled onto the shoulder and stopped. The sunset was beautiful. It was just a road, he told himself. Roads could be confusing. Signs could be wrong. People got tired.

He started driving again, beneath the darkening trees, and a message appeared where the story of his journey should have been.

YOU ARE MAKING GOOD PROGRESS.

He waited. Nothing else appeared. The narration continued, and Elias drove on, telling himself that exhaustion could make a person imagine almost anything. Somewhere before dawn the road bent toward a town he knew, and he took the exit without deciding to.

## The Delivery Sheet | 1.04-1.46 | wrong
*Day 2. Bright and wrong. The records run ahead of him.*

Morning came without asking. The sheet on the dashboard said the next parcel, Orchard Row, had already been delivered at 18:04. It was not even noon.

Elias stood at the address and looked at the door. There was a package on the step, the same size as the one in his van. The label was in his own handwriting. He did not touch it.

He drove back toward the highway with the windows down. The countryside was bright and soft, and nothing in it explained anything. He told himself the sheet was wrong. Sheets were wrong all the time.

The road climbed a gentle hill before descending into a wide valley. From the top, he could see miles of cottages and narrow lanes. It was beautiful. He could not remember when he had last stopped to look at it.

He passed a small roadside shop with flower boxes beneath its windows. A handwritten sign advertised fresh bread. He had passed this shop yesterday, in the same light, at the same speed. He was sure of it. He was less sure of yesterday.

His day began to run a little ahead of him. Elias braked for a tractor before the tractor appeared. He knew the next bend before the road reached it. By the time the afternoon softened, the van knew the way better than he did.

## A Place Between Places | 1.52-1.84 | wrong
*Night 2. The road speaks. Someone has already finished the route.*

Mill Lane again. The sheet said it was delivered, though Elias had not signed for it a second time. He sat outside the first house on his route and listened to the engine tick. The porch light was on. Nobody came to the door.

He started driving again. The highway narrowed between two rows of trees, and the sky deepened toward violet. A message appeared.

YOUR DESTINATION IS STILL AHEAD.

The road emerged from the trees into open countryside. There were no houses now, no farm tracks, no distant town lights. Only the highway and the dark fields beyond it. The horizon seemed farther away than before.

Elias remembered stories he had read once, years ago, about roads that were not supposed to exist. Roads that appeared after dark. Journeys that lasted for days without crossing another town. He had laughed at those stories. Now he wished he could remember how they ended.

YOU HAVE BEEN HERE BEFORE.

He had never been here before. He was certain of it. The next message arrived before the thought had finished forming.

YOU WERE ALWAYS GOING TO COME HERE.

@act horror
Elias looked at the package beside him. He had accepted it at the depot, loaded it into the van, and followed the printed address. He tried to remember who had handed it to him. He couldn't. He tried to remember what it was meant to contain. He couldn't remember that, either.

THE DELIVERY MUST BE COMPLETED.

Somewhere ahead, someone had already completed it. Elias knew that the way he knew his own name, which was to say without being able to say how. He took the exit. He did not remember choosing it.

## The Road Remembers | 2.62-2.94 | horror
*Night 3, then the false dawn. The nightmare, the peaceful morning, the empty back seat.*

The depot lot was empty. In the bay where he had parked on the first morning, the tarmac was warm in the shape of a van, though nothing had stood there for days. Elias sat very still. Then he pulled out onto the highway, because there was nowhere else to go.

The road had no exit. Elias drove until the fields gave way to hills, until the hills gave way to forests, until the forests opened onto landscapes he couldn't have named. The world changed around him, but the highway remained.

He passed places that felt almost familiar. A little town beneath a church steeple. A stone bridge over a dry riverbed. A row of houses with warm windows. Each place seemed to belong somewhere else. None of them led anywhere.

The road was a journey without a proper beginning or end, a place that existed between destinations. It borrowed the shapes of the world because that was all it knew how to do. Fields. Houses. Towns. Sunsets. It could imitate everything a traveler expected to find, but it could not understand why any of it mattered.

A house was just walls and windows. A town was just buildings beside a road. And home was just another destination printed on a label.

YOU HAVE FOLLOWED EVERY DIRECTION.

YOU HAVE COMPLETED EVERY MILE.

Elias looked at the delivery sheet. The count was in the thousands. Every entry was signed in his handwriting, and every one of them was dated before he had arrived.

DELIVERY RECIPIENT: IDENTIFIED.

The road had not been taking him to a place. It had been taking him to the end of the journey itself. And the package was not something he had been carrying. It was the reason he had been allowed to keep going. The light went out of the windscreen all at once.

@act finale
Elias woke to birdsong and a thin gold light across the dashboard. He was in the van, parked in the first bay of the depot lot, the window cracked open. A nightmare. Of course it had been a nightmare. His neck ached the way necks ache after a night in a seat.

For one brief moment it was an ordinary morning. The fields glowed gold, the air smelled of cut grass, and the world seemed full of small kindnesses and ordinary chances to begin again. He thought about his living room, his work boots by the door, a simple dinner.

Then he noticed the delivery sheet on the dashboard. The count had not gone back to zero. Every record was still there, signed, dated, complete.

He glanced in the mirror, to check his own face. The back seat was empty. He looked at it for a long time.

[break]
It was empty yesterday, too.
