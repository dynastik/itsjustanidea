# itsjustanidea: Story Script

## Act I: The Last Delivery
*Tone: Warm, peaceful, ordinary. Afternoon sunlight.*

The afternoon sun rested over a quiet stretch of countryside. Green fields rolled toward distant hills, and clouds drifted lazily across the sky.

Elias had one delivery left.

It had been an uneventful day, which was exactly how he liked his working days. No traffic jams, no angry customers, no packages sent to the wrong address. Just a long drive through the countryside and the promise of an evening with absolutely nothing to do.

He had already decided what he would have for dinner. Something simple. He would leave his work boots by the door, open a window, and watch the sunset from his living room.

It was a small thing to look forward to, but small things had a way of making an ordinary day feel complete.

The road curved gently between the fields. A wooden sign pointed toward a nearby town, and a farmhouse stood beyond a line of trees. Somewhere in the distance, smoke rose from a chimney.

Elias smiled to himself.

One more delivery, and the day was his.

The address on the package led farther into the countryside than he expected. Still, there was nothing particularly unusual about that. People lived in quiet places. Roads went where roads went.

He followed the highway beneath the open sky.

For a while, the world was exactly as it should be.

## Act II: A Long, Pleasant Drive
*Tone: Still cozy. Late afternoon begins to soften.*

The fields gradually gave way to low stone walls and scattered houses. Sunlight caught the windows of passing farmhouses, turning them gold.

Elias had always liked driving through places like these. There was something reassuring about a road that stretched toward the horizon, about knowing that somewhere ahead there would be a town, and beyond that, another road leading home.

He passed a small roadside shop with flower boxes beneath its windows. A handwritten sign outside advertised fresh bread.

For a moment, he wondered whether he should have stopped to buy something for dinner.

Then he remembered that he had already decided what to eat.

He laughed quietly at himself and kept driving.

The afternoon wore on. The sky turned a softer shade of blue, and the shadows of the hills lengthened across the fields.

Elias checked the delivery label at a stop along the road.

The address was still the same.

He tucked the package away and continued.

The road climbed a gentle hill before descending into a wide valley. From the top, he could see miles of countryside, dotted with cottages and narrow lanes.

It was beautiful.

He wished he could stay out here a little longer, without a destination, without a schedule, without anyone waiting for him.

Then he remembered the dinner waiting at home.

There was something lovely about having somewhere to return to.

He drove on.

## Act III: The Wrong Way
*Tone: The first undeniable contradictions. Early evening.*

The sun had begun to sink toward the hills when Elias saw a sign beside the road.

WELCOME TO BELLWEATHER.

He didn't recognize the name, but that wasn't surprising. He had taken unfamiliar roads before.

A smaller sign beneath it gave the distance to the town.

Twelve miles.

Elias continued along the highway.

The road bent around a wooded hill, passed a stretch of open farmland, and descended into a shallow valley.

Then another sign appeared.

WELCOME TO BELLWEATHER.

Elias frowned.

The lettering was different, and the sign looked newer than the first. But the name was unmistakable.

He checked the road behind him in the mirror. There had been no turn. No junction. No reason he could think of to have circled back.

He kept driving.

Perhaps the first sign had marked the wider district. Perhaps the second marked the town itself. There were plenty of reasonable explanations for things, and Elias had never been fond of inventing unreasonable ones.

A few minutes later, he passed a roadside milestone.

Twelve miles to Bellweather.

He slowed slightly.

The road ahead remained empty. The countryside was quiet, the sky peaceful, and the last sunlight lay across the fields like a blanket.

Nothing looked wrong.

That was what bothered him.

Elias looked down at the delivery label again. The address had not changed, but the printed destination was unfamiliar now in a way he couldn't explain. He had read it several times that afternoon. He was certain of that.

He simply couldn't remember the name.

The road descended into the valley.

The sun touched the horizon.

And the destination remained twelve miles away.

## Act IV: A Place Between Places
*Tone: Dusk. Unease becomes supernatural horror.*

Elias pulled onto the shoulder and stopped.

For the first time that day, he wasn't sure what to do next.

He could turn around. He could keep going. He could call someone, if he could think of anyone who would understand why a road had stopped making sense.

But there was no signal, and no other cars passed.

He looked out across the fields.

The sunset was beautiful. Orange light spread beneath the clouds, and the distant hills stood in dark silhouette. For a moment, he felt embarrassed by his own fear.

It was just a road.

Roads could be confusing. Signs could be wrong. People got tired.

He started driving again.

The highway narrowed between two rows of trees. The last light faded from the fields, and the sky deepened toward violet.

A message appeared.

YOU ARE MAKING GOOD PROGRESS.

Elias stared at the words.

They were not on a road sign. They were not printed on the package. They appeared where the story of his journey should have been, calm and matter-of-fact, as if someone had interrupted a conversation to offer a harmless observation.

He waited.

Nothing else appeared.

Then the narration continued.

Elias drove beneath the darkening trees. He told himself that exhaustion could make a person imagine almost anything.

Another message appeared.

YOUR DESTINATION IS STILL AHEAD.

He pressed his foot against the accelerator.

The road emerged from the trees into open countryside. There were no houses now, no farm tracks, no distant town lights. Only the highway and the dark fields beyond it.

The horizon seemed farther away than before.

Elias remembered something he had read once, years ago, about travelers who lost their way on roads that weren't supposed to exist. Stories told in passing, usually as jokes. Roads that appeared after dark. Journeys that lasted for days without crossing another town. People who drove toward a destination they could never reach.

He had laughed at those stories.

Now he wished he could remember how they ended.

A third message appeared.

YOU HAVE BEEN HERE BEFORE.

Elias shook his head.

He had never been here before.

He was certain of it.

The next message arrived before the previous thought had finished forming.

YOU WERE ALWAYS GOING TO COME HERE.

The road continued beneath the headlights.

Elias looked at the package beside him.

He had been carrying it all afternoon. He had accepted it at the depot, loaded it into the van, and followed the printed address. It was an ordinary delivery. An ordinary job.

He tried to remember who had handed it to him.

He couldn't.

He tried to remember what the package was meant to contain.

He couldn't remember that, either.

A new message appeared.

THE DELIVERY MUST BE COMPLETED.

Elias gripped the steering wheel.

For the first time, he understood that the road wasn't simply leading him somewhere unfamiliar.

It was keeping him from going anywhere else.

## Act V: The Road Remembers
*Tone: Deep night. The supernatural truth becomes clear, without a full lore dump.*

The road had no exit.

Elias drove until the fields gave way to hills, until the hills gave way to forests, until the forests opened onto landscapes he couldn't have named.

The world changed around him, but the highway remained.

He passed places that felt almost familiar. A little town beneath a church steeple. A stone bridge over a dry riverbed. A row of houses with warm windows.

Each place seemed to belong somewhere else.

None of them led anywhere.

He began to understand what the stories had meant.

The road wasn't a road in the ordinary sense. It was a journey without a proper beginning or end, a place that existed between destinations. It borrowed the shapes of the world because that was all it knew how to do.

Fields. Houses. Towns. Sunsets.

It could imitate the things a traveler expected to find along the way.

But it couldn't understand why those things mattered.

A house was just walls and windows. A town was just buildings beside a road. A sunset was just light disappearing beyond the horizon.

And home was just another destination printed on a label.

Elias looked at the package.

He finally understood why it had been given to him.

It had never been intended for an ordinary recipient. The road had given him a delivery to complete because a journey needed a purpose. A destination gave a traveler a reason to keep moving.

Without one, perhaps he would have stopped long ago.

The messages returned.

YOU HAVE FOLLOWED EVERY DIRECTION.

YOU HAVE COMPLETED EVERY MILE.

YOU HAVE REACHED THE END OF THE ROAD.

Elias looked ahead.

The highway stretched into darkness.

Then the final message appeared.

DELIVERY RECIPIENT: IDENTIFIED.

Elias felt a cold certainty settle over him.

The road had not been taking him to a place.

It had been taking him to the end of the journey itself.

And the package was not something he had been carrying.

It was the reason he had been allowed to keep going.

## Act VI: The Last Sunset
*Tone: Quiet, beautiful, bittersweet. The horror gives way to a false peace.*

The sky began to brighten.

At first, Elias thought it was dawn. A pale line of gold appeared along the horizon, slowly spreading across the hills.

The road straightened.

The fields returned, green and peaceful beneath the morning light. A farmhouse stood in the distance, smoke rising from its chimney. The world seemed to have settled into place again.

Elias eased his foot off the accelerator.

For the first time in what felt like an eternity, he could breathe.

Perhaps it was over.

Perhaps there had been an explanation all along, and he simply hadn't been able to see it.

The package lay beside him, quiet and ordinary.

A message appeared.

DELIVERY ACCEPTED.

Elias watched the sunrise.

He thought about his living room. His work boots by the door. The simple dinner he had planned to eat. The window he would open to let in the evening air.

He wondered whether he would ever see any of those things again.

The sun rose higher.

The fields glowed gold, and the road ahead looked warm and inviting. It was the kind of morning that made a person believe the world was a good place, full of small kindnesses and ordinary chances to begin again.

For one brief moment, Elias believed he was going home.

Then another message appeared.

THANK YOU FOR BRINGING YOURSELF.

The light grew brighter.

Elias kept driving.

The road continued toward the horizon, smooth and empty beneath the beautiful sky.

He didn't know whether the journey had ended.

He only knew that he had reached somewhere, and that for the first time since the afternoon began, he no longer felt afraid.

The screen faded to black.
